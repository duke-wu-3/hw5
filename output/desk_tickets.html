Reflection

Reflection (Problem 10)

1. How do you evaluate the performance of the agents on each ticket and why?

I would rank this around 7/10. Although the agents were able to solve each ticket properly, they were acting inefficiently (using around 245k tokens for relatively simple tasks).

2. For each ticket, how did actual compare to the expected plan you wrote earlier?

Per ticket, I think the actual runs matched the plan around a 6/10. There were times where they needed to conduct multiple runs leading to differences. I think this is because not all of the agents have access to the same information.

3. What would have been simpler as one agent with tools and why?

I think this entire project would have been simpler as one agent with all of the tools. These tickets are relatively simple to resolve – and we ended up causing a large amount of inefficiency by adding different agents with different levels of access. I think the way I’d have designed the system is by adding in guardrails to ensure that shared information was only ever stored / seen by the system, or if we still want subagents, consolidating them based off function / the type of info they have access to. This way there would be no possibility of information improperly leaking to users.

4. Three new problems Campus Customs might face that this agent team could solve with the tools you built

I think there are three new problems the team could solve with the tools we already built:

  • A customer wants an item that’s out of stock, like a crest mug. Inventory can check stock, find the right vendor (Elm City Gifts instead of Bulldog Print), and quote the restock with an arrival date. Accounting can confirm we have the cash, and Customer Service drafts the reply – I’d just need to sign the purchase order. This is basically ticket 101 with a different vendor.
  • Another club asks for a bulk discount, e.g. 25 tees at 20% off. Accounting can test the discount and find the most we can give before selling at cost, and counter with a safer rate if 20% is too deep. Inventory prices any restock, and I approve the final rate. This is basically ticket 103.
  • A vendor invoice is about to go overdue. Accounting can rank every open bill and rent payment by urgency and queue the payment before the vendor stops shipping to us. This would catch the problem from ticket 101 before it happens.

5. Three new problems this agent team could not solve with the tools you built, and the tools and agents you would need

I think there are three problems the team could not solve with the current tools:

  • October rent ($2,400) is due Oct 2, and we only have $152 in checking. The team can see the shortfall, but nothing brings money in – there are no sales, customer payment, or credit tools. I’d add tools to record sales and collect customer payments (like a deposit on the AI Club’s $1,044 hoodie order), plus an email tool so Facilities could ask the landlord for an extension. I’d also add a new Sales / Revenue agent to own income.
  • A customer returns a defective hoodie and wants a refund. There’s no customer or order history to confirm the purchase, no way to send money back, and nothing adds the hoodie back into inventory. I’d add customer and order tables, a refund tool that I’d still sign off on, and a tool to update inventory. Customer Service would handle returns, and Accounting would approve refunds.
  • Planning stock for a busy period like move-in week or homecoming. There’s no sales history or forecasting, and the agents can’t change prices or add products – they can only react to one ticket at a time. I’d add sales history, demand forecasting, competitor price search, and pricing / product tools, with a new Merchandising agent planning purchases ahead of time.
